How we verify audio

A FLAC label
isn’t proof.

Flackey looks inside every file before accepting it. A renamed or upscaled track doesn’t pass just because its filename says FLAC.

  1. 01

    Confirm the recording.

    When a Deezer preview is available, Chromaprint compares its audio fingerprint with the downloaded file to make sure it’s the same track and version.

  2. 02

    Inspect the spectrum.

    Flackey analyzes the frequencies in the audio to spot a lossy file disguised inside a lossless container.

  3. 03

    Enforce the quality floor.

    Fake lossless files and MP3s below 320 kbps are rejected. You get the best verified source Flackey can find.

About Flackey

We didn’t reinvent music sharing.
We made it easier.

Flackey is powered by Soulseek, an independent, community-powered network where music lovers share rare and high-quality recordings directly with one another. Flackey takes part in that exchange by sharing your downloaded tracks back with the network. When a lossless copy isn’t available, it falls back to a verified 320 kbps MP3 from Deezer.

We didn’t invent the wheel—we’re making these great sources more accessible. Flackey will grow around what its users actually ask for.

Installing Flackey
  1. Download and open Flackey.pkg.
  2. macOS will block it the first time — see below. This is expected.
  3. Follow the macOS Installer steps. Flackey is placed in Applications.
  4. Open Flackey and connect Telegram, Soulseek, or both.

macOS will say it can’t verify Flackey

Flackey isn’t notarized yet, which needs a paid Apple Developer account. macOS blocks anything unnotarized by default, so you’ll see this the first time you open the installer:

Then allow it once:

  1. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security, and scroll down to Security.
  2. Next to “Flackey.pkg was blocked to protect your Mac,” click Open Anyway.
  3. Authenticate with Touch ID or your password.
  4. Click Open Anyway once more when the warning comes back. The installer then runs normally.

If you don’t see an “Open Anyway” button, it has expired — it only appears for about an hour after macOS blocks something. Double-click Flackey.pkg again, click Done, and go straight back to Privacy & Security.

On macOS 14 and earlier you could control-click the installer and choose Open. macOS 15 removed that, so Privacy & Security is now the only route.

Rather not take our word for it? Flackey is open source — you can read the code and build it yourself.
