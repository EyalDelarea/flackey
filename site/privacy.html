Privacy

Usage stats, and what they never include.

Flackey can share usage stats that help us find out why downloads fail. They are off unless you turn them on. We can’t see what you download: we only get counts like “a Soulseek search found nothing”, never song names, links or files.

Applies from Flackey 0.7.0, the first version with usage stats.

Off unless you say yes

Setup’s Welcome screen shows a note from me, “Hey! Eyal here 👋”, asking you to share usage stats, with two equal answers, and neither is picked for you. If you already use Flackey, it shows the same note on the main screen until you answer. Not now, closing the question or not answering all leave stats off.

You can change your mind at any time in Settings › Privacy. While stats are off, nothing is collected or sent.

What is sent

Short, fixed facts about how the app is doing:

  • Your setup: the Flackey version, your operating system and its major version, whether the computer is Apple silicon/ARM or Intel/AMD, and how Flackey was installed.
  • Setup and settings: which setup steps you finished or skipped, and broad choices like the file format you save in or how your library folders are laid out.
  • Downloads: the kind of request (typed text, or a YouTube or Spotify track or playlist), whether it finished or failed, at which step, and which download route it took.
  • Quality checks: the result, the claimed file format, and the sample rate and bit depth in broad groups.
  • Health: whether Soulseek and Telegram are connected, how long the app ran, and whether it crashed.
  • Errors: the type of error and where in Flackey’s own code it happened. Never the error message.
  • Updates: whether an update check, download or install worked, whether it started by itself or by you, how long it took as a range, and the Flackey version it was updating to.
  • Your actions and library: what you did with a download, such as retry, clear or keep anyway; your library’s size as a range; and whether Soulseek’s port is reachable.

Times and sizes are sent as ranges like “1-5s”. Small counts, like how many times a download was retried, are sent as exact numbers.

Never sent: song, artist or label names, search text, links, file names or folders, your Soulseek or Telegram account details, error messages or logs.

See exactly what is sent

In Settings › Privacy, See exactly what’s sent lists every kind of report and every field it can carry. The list is read from the same rules our stats server checks each report against, so it can’t promise less than is sent. Privacy details there opens this page.

What’s sent, just below it, shows the exact reports waiting to go and the most recent ones that went, as they leave your computer.

Your random ID and secret

When you turn stats on, Flackey makes a random ID on your computer. It isn’t linked to your name, email, computer or any account. It only tells us that two reports came from the same copy of Flackey. Each time the app starts it also makes a random session ID, so reports from one run can be grouped.

With the ID, Flackey makes a random secret. It stays on your computer and goes only to our stats server, with each batch of reports and with a delete request. It proves a request comes from your copy of Flackey, so someone who learns your ID can’t delete your stats or send stats in your name. It is never shown on screen, and it’s left out of bug reports. Our server keeps only a one-way fingerprint (a hash) of it.

New ID in Settings › Privacy replaces both, and deletes what was sent under the old ID.

Your IP address

Flackey’s stats server never stores or logs your IP address. Like most websites, the network in front of it (Cloudflare and our hosting firewall) sees IP addresses to block attacks, keeps them for up to 7 days, and keeps a short list of the busiest addresses for up to 400 days. Your ID is never in the web address or headers those logs keep, and we don’t link those logs to your stats.

The stats database has no place to store an IP address, and every report is checked against a fixed list of allowed fields and values before it is stored.

How long we keep it

  • The detailed reports are deleted automatically after 180 days.
  • After that we keep only daily totals for groups of 5 or more installs, with no ID, for up to 2 years. We also keep a daily count of how many reports of each kind arrived, with no ID, for up to 2 years.
  • We don’t make backups of the detailed reports.

Turning it off and deleting your data

Turn off Share usage stats in Settings › Privacy. Flackey stops sending straight away, clears the reports still waiting on your computer, and asks our server to erase everything sent under your ID.

  • Our server erases it at its next hourly cleanup, so within about an hour of receiving the request.
  • If Flackey can’t reach the server, it keeps trying, even after a restart, until the server confirms. Settings › Privacy shows where it stands.
  • The server keeps a hash of the deleted ID for 30 days, so it can finish the job and drop any late reports sent under that ID.
  • Daily totals already archived from reports older than 180 days still count your install, as one of at least 5, with no ID. Nothing in them points to you, so they can’t be removed.

Not part of usage stats

The update check asks GitHub for new releases. Bug reports are only sent when you press Send in Report a problem: the report you reviewed goes to our server, without your usage-stats ID, whether or not usage stats are on, and is deleted after 90 days. You can choose to email it instead.
